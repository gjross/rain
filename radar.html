How this works
  • Radar (0–2 h). The last 30 minutes of NEXRAD 5-minute mosaics around you, 400 km across. Storm motion is measured by matching echoes between scans 15 minutes apart, then the current rain field is pushed forward along that motion. 60 slightly-perturbed runs (speed, direction, position) give the chance: the share of runs with rain (≥18 dBZ, about 0.5 mm/h) over your spot. It cannot create new storms. Pop-up cells that haven't formed yet won't show here.
  • HRRR (0–12 h). NOAA's 3 km storm-resolving model at 15-minute steps, sampled at 17 points out to 20 km. The orange line is the share of those points with rain within ±15 min. That's a neighborhood chance, because a storm forecast 8 km off is still a useful forecast. The intensity strip is HRRR right at your spot.
  • NBM. The National Blend of Models' calibrated hourly chance of rain, close to what the NWS forecast says.
  • Pulls. Nothing loads until you tap a button. "Next useful refresh" is when new data that could change the answer is due: the next radar scan when rain is close, otherwise the next HRRR run.
